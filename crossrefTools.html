Crossref Connectivity

Measures how well authors, editors, affiliations, and funders carry the identifiers that connect them — and shows how your metadata has changed over time.

  • ORCID, ROR, and funder-identifier coverage at a glance
  • Drill-downs list every unidentified name — quick wins first

Retrieve · Organizations

ROR Retriever

Matches affiliation and funder names to ROR identifiers using the same affiliation-matching strategies DataCite, Crossref, and ROR use.

  • Careful algorithmic matching first, full query search as fallback
  • Color-coded confidence with full candidate details
  • CSV results ready for analysis and re-curation

Retrieve · People

ORCID Retriever

Finds ORCID iDs for lists of people, showing each candidate’s most recent employment and journal so the right person is easy to pick.

  • Handles “First Last” and “Last, First” name forms
  • Built-in disambiguation for common names
  • CSV results ready for analysis and re-curation

The tools work as a pipeline. Measure a journal’s or member’s completeness and connectivity → drill down to the specific people and organizations missing identifiers → retrieve their RORs and ORCIDs with one click → re-curate your Crossref deposits and watch the coverage improve. Every hand-off carries its context, so you always know which selection you are improving. The Crossref Guide walks you through the whole loop.
Crossref scores are not comparable to DataCite scores. The two ecosystems carry different metadata, so a Crossref FAIR total is measured against a smaller, Crossref-expressible catalog. Compare a Crossref member to another Crossref member — and see the Crossref Guide for the concept mappings and the four documented approximations.
